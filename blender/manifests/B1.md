## Asset 1: market (L1) — sandbox/assets/models/market.glb

**What it is (stranger line):** A Victorian glass-and-iron covered market hall,
open at the sides between brick piers, with a big arched glazed roof.

**Functional parts planned:**
- Joined: brick piers built up from a plinth course + shaft + moulded impost
  band the arches spring from; iron columns bolted to a cast base plate,
  capital bracket where the roof truss lands.
- Supported: roof trusses (bottom chord + diagonal struts) carry the glazing
  bars; glazing bars carry individual glass panes; lantern sits on its own
  raised clerestory wall carried by the ridge trusses.
- Moves/opens: entrance gate posts with hinge plates implied (gates read as
  open, hinge knuckles + strap visible on the posts).
- Gripped/carried: n/a (no hand tools) — worn threshold step at doorway is the
  human-contact point instead.
- Fixed down: piers on a continuous plinth; columns on base plates; downpipes
  strapped to piers at intervals.
- Weathered: soot-darkened brick (dark variant of London stock), worn
  threshold stone, rust bleed at iron joints.
- Maintained: gutters + downpipes actually route from eaves to ground (not
  floating), ridge ventilation lantern has its own small roof so it sheds
  water instead of being a slot.

## Asset 2: market-stall — stall.glb

**What it is:** A collapsible trestle market stall with a canvas awning over
a plank counter, holding produce boxes.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (A-frame trestle legs cross-halved and
pegged, top boards resting on trestle rails with visible gaps between
boards), supported (canopy poles socketed into the trestle frame, canvas tied
to the poles with visible tie points, not fused to them), moves (A-frames
imply a folding joint at the cross), gripped (side rail as a hand rail along
the front), fixed (canopy pole feet sit in a socket/bracket), weathered
(canvas sag/fold under its own tension), maintained (crates stacked, not
melted into the boards).

## Asset 3: crate — crate.glb

**What it is:** A slatted wooden produce crate.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (boards nailed to corner battens, visible
nail heads, gaps between boards), supported (battens are the load path at the
corners, thicker than the slats), gripped (hand-holes cut through both end

## Asset 4: barrel — barrel.glb

**What it is:** A wooden cask/barrel with iron hoops.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (staves meet edge to edge around the
bulge, chime bevel at both ends where the head sits in the croze groove),
supported/fixed (3 iron hoops standing proud of the stave surface at chime,

## Asset 5: sack-pile — sacks.glb

**What it is:** A pile of 2-3 slumped jute sacks with tied necks.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (tied neck = twist + wrap read as
geometry, not a texture), supported (sacks slump and flatten where they rest
on the ground and on each other — different silhouette per sack), weathered
(creases at every contact point), material named `cloth` per brief override.

## Round 1 judge verdict: FAIL

Independent judge scored round 1 as FAIL (batch score = min asset score = 1,
from sacks; bar is every asset >=4). Scores: market 4, crate 4, stall 3,
barrel 3, sacks 1. Two ruling notes were accepted with no action needed: the
plinth bound-box disclosure (85.7x45.7 m footprint vs nominal 84x44 m) is
ACCEPTED — wall envelope at 84x44 governs; the 12 m entrance per README stands
over the plate's printed "45'0"" figure — that pack contradiction is logged
against the source pack, not against this build.

The judge's ordered fix list, and what round 2 did about each item, follows.

### 1. sacks — REBUILD from a multi-lobe deformed body

Judge: "REBUILD each sack from a multi-lobe deformed body (several
overlapping squashed lumps, flattened at ground/mutual contacts), plus a
modeled gathered neck: twist + wrap band + ear above the tie. (Frames read as
spinning tops/gongs; 'What is it?' not answerable.)"

This is exactly the multi-lobe fix already named as the fallback in this
manifest's own round-1 caveat — the judge held the build to its own stated
next step. `build_sacks.py` was rewritten so each sack is 3-4 overlapping
squashed icospheres (base lump, second lump, shoulder lump), not a lathed
rotational profile. Each sack gets a short stubby tapered neck, a wrap-band
ring at the tie point, and an "ear" lump above the wrap overlapping it (not
held out on a stalk, which read as a spike in the first rebuild attempt). Tris
1,092 (< 8k prop budget).

### 2. barrel — REBUILD the top end + stave joints + bung + hoops

Judge: "REBUILD the top end: continuous staves to the chime, head seated
inside a croze, no picket-gap rim tips; ADD vertical stave joint lines around
the body; MODEL the bung boss on the bilge; replace the hoop-seam lump with a
riveted lap. Re-render face/34 with the WHOLE barrel in frame (both currently
crop it)."

`build_barrel.py`: the stave profile now ends at z=0.85 (was 0.90), leaving
room for a proper croze collar (a hollow standing ring built via a new
`add_tube()` helper — `add_cyl()` always caps solid, so it couldn't leave a
visible rim opening) plus a recessed head disk sitting inside it. Stave joint
lines now follow the actual bulge profile per PROFILE segment instead of one
straight rod at the mean radius (the round-1 defect that poked past the chime
and read as picket-fence tips). The bung moved to z=0.58, clear of all three
hoops (round 1 had it coincide with the middle hoop, reading as one lump).
Each hoop gets a riveted lap joint (a strap plate + 2 proud rivet nubs via a
new `add_rivet()` helper) instead of a seamless ring. Both `cam_face` and
`cam_34` were pulled back (z-target 0.45, distances 2.4 m / 2.0 m at lens 50)
so the whole barrel including the collar and head is in frame — round 1
cropped the top. Tris 6,580 (< 8k prop budget).

### 3. stall — REBUILD canopy as draped canvas + real crates + fix peg/artifact

Judge: "REBUILD the canopy as draped canvas: continuous sag between poles,
visible tie wraps at pole heads; REBUILD the counter goods as actual crates
(reuse crate.glb scaled once fixed); make the X-crossing peg readable; remove
the black rod artifact from stall_face."

`build_canopy()` was rewritten from flat unrotated panel segments (the source
of the black-rod artifact) into one continuous swept surface: an explicit
vertex-ring sweep along the pole-to-pole axis with a `sin`-curve sag and real
thickness, closed with top/bottom/edge faces and end caps at the poles — no
segment joints, no seams. Two tie-wrap rings per pole mark where the canvas is
lashed on. `crate.py`'s geometry was factored into a reusable `add_crate()`
function and `build_stall.py` now embeds two real (nail-less, for tri budget)
crates on the counter via `from build_crate import add_crate` instead of
placeholder boxes. The crossing peg was fattened from 0.018 m to 0.03 m radius
plus two washer boxes added at the crossing faces. Bevel segments dropped from
2 to 1 to bring the tri count from 9,368 back under the 8k cap after adding
the swept canopy and embedded crates — final 3,904 tris.

### 4. crate — REBUILD top course with legible hand-holes

Judge: "REBUILD the top course: symmetric stacking horns or consistent slat
spacing, and cut legible hand-holes visible in at least one frame."

The hand-hole was previously the topmost board split in two, open to the sky
above it — illegible as a hole rather than a gap. `add_crate()` now cuts the
hand-hole one row below the top (`hole_row = n_boards - 2`), bounded by a
material strip above and below the opening plus two side segments, so it
reads as a closed rectangle with a complete, uncut board still forming the
top edge. `cam_detail` was repositioned to shoot straight at the hole's end
face. This also closes the judge's first clause ("symmetric stacking horns or
consistent slat spacing"): the top course is now a complete uncut board, with
the four corner battens standing symmetrically proud above it, clearly
visible in `crate_34.png`. Tris 4,264 (< 8k prop budget).

### 5. market — truss depth, gable glazed screen, arch form, gable bay count,
   impost band

Judge: "ADD truss depth (bottom chord + diagonals as real members, not
lines), ADD the semicircular glazed gable screen per the tier1 plate, correct
arch form to semicircular and gable-end bay count to the plate's arcade, add
the impost band the arches spring from. (Protects its score of 4.)"

Five separate fixes in `build_market.py`:
- **Arch form**: `arch_profile()` rewritten to a true semicircular soffit
  (`math.cos`/`math.sin` sweep, radius = span/2), replacing the round-1
  parabolic-bulge approximation. Long-wall spring at z=3.6 (span 10.6,
  crown 8.9), entrance spring at z=2.8 (span 12.0, crown 8.8), flank spring
  at z=2.0 (span ~13.9, crown 8.95) — all crowns land at or under the 9.0 m
  eave.
- **Gable-end bay count**: `build_short_wall()` simplified from a 5-bay
  composition (extra piers at +-14, two arches per flank) to the plate's
  3-bay arrangement — one large flank arch, the entrance, one large flank
  arch — by removing the extra pier pair and widening each flank arch to a
  single span.
- **Truss depth**: `build_trusses()` rewritten from single-line bottom
  chords and rafters to real triangulated depth — fattened bottom tie and
  rafter chords, vertical members every ~3 m from tie to rafter, and a
  zigzag diagonal web between them, per truss station.
- **Semicircular glazed gable screen**: a true semicircular iron rib (radius
  6.85, spring at RIDGE_Z - 6.85 = 7.15) added inside the gable above the
  entrance, built the same `cos`/`sin` sweep as the wall arches, with a
  spring-line band it rests on.
- **Impost band**: first attempt (during this round) centered the band on
  each bay opening, sized to the full span — this put a stone bar across
  every arch including the 12 m entrance, a new defect rather than the
  requested fix. Caught before the shipping render and corrected: bands are
  now placed per-pier, sized to the pier's own width (PIER_W + 0.25), so they
  project past the pier face as a lintel course and never bridge an opening.
  Jamb piers (shared between the entrance arch and a flank arch) get two
  bands stacked at their two different spring heights (2.0 and 2.8) instead
  of one band trying to serve both springs.

Also fixed in the same pass: the entrance keystone's z was still computed
from a stale hardcoded value from the old arch profile; recalculated as
`ENTRANCE_SPRING + ENTRANCE_HALF + 0.35` = 9.15, matching the new crown at
8.8. Final tris 57,156 (< 60,000 building budget, checked directly against
the built object, not carried over from an earlier estimate).

## Verdicts (filled in after round-2 render review)

### Asset 1: market — sandbox/assets/models/market.glb

Renders: `blender/renders/B1/market_face.png`, `market_34.png`,
`market_detail.png`, `market_ctx.png`, `market_interior.png`. Tris: 57,156
(< 60k building budget). GLB re-verified by fresh reimport: materials
`['brick', 'glass', 'iron', 'slate', 'stone']`, no `.001` duplicates, bound
dims 85.7 x 45.7 x 15.8 m (footprint disclosure already accepted by the
round-1 judge).

**Stranger answers, from the frames alone:**
- *What is it?* A large covered market hall — glass-and-iron gable roof over
  an open brick arcade of true semicircular arches, with a 3-bay grand
  arched entrance elevation at each end (`market_face`, `market_ctx`).
- *How is it built?* Brick piers on a plinth, each carrying a projecting
  stone impost course at the exact height its arch springs from, support
  semicircular arches along the open sides and across the gable ends; inside,
  iron columns carry roof trusses with real triangulated depth — fattened
  top/bottom chords, vertical posts and a zigzag diagonal web — visible from
  underneath (`market_interior`, `market_34`). A semicircular glazed iron
  screen fills the gable above the entrance.
- *How is it used?* You walk in through the 12 m semicircular-arched
  entrance, past iron gate posts with hinge plates and over a worn threshold
  step, into an open hall lit through the glass roof and the gable screen
  (`market_detail`, `market_face`).

Not fully resolved: the interior columns' exact structural logic (why the
roof needs mid-span support at y=+-10 specifically) remains this build's own
interpretation, since the brief states the column position but not the
reason. The gable-end radiating glazing bars are a simplification of the
reference plate's ornate ironwork density, matching function (real glazing
bars, now plus the semicircular screen the judge required) but not the
plate's full decorative scrollwork — printed brief figures were followed over
the plate's aesthetic detail per BRIEF-COMMON's own rule.
