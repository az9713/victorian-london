# Batch B1 — Spitalfields market building + market props

Draft functional-parts list written before geometry (per BRIEF-COMMON step 4).
Verdicts and stranger-answers filled in after clay renders are reviewed.

## Asset 1: market (L1) — sandbox/assets/models/market.glb

**What it is (stranger line):** A Victorian glass-and-iron covered market hall,
open at the sides between brick piers, with a big arched glazed roof.

**Functional parts planned:**
- Joined: brick piers built up from a plinth course + shaft + moulded impost
  band the arches spring from; iron columns bolted to a cast base plate,
  capital bracket where the roof truss lands.
- Supported: roof trusses (bottom chord + diagonal struts) carry the glazing
  bars; glazing bars carry individual glass panes; lantern sits on its own
  raised clerestory wall carried by the ridge trusses.
- Moves/opens: entrance gate posts with hinge plates implied (gates read as
  open, hinge knuckles + strap visible on the posts).
- Gripped/carried: n/a (no hand tools) — worn threshold step at doorway is the
  human-contact point instead.
- Fixed down: piers on a continuous plinth; columns on base plates; downpipes
  strapped to piers at intervals.
- Weathered: soot-darkened brick (dark variant of London stock), worn
  threshold stone, rust bleed at iron joints.
- Maintained: gutters + downpipes actually route from eaves to ground (not
  floating), ridge ventilation lantern has its own small roof so it sheds
  water instead of being a slot.

## Asset 2: market-stall — stall.glb

**What it is:** A collapsible trestle market stall with a canvas awning over
a plank counter, holding produce boxes.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (A-frame trestle legs cross-halved and
pegged, top boards resting on trestle rails with visible gaps between
boards), supported (canopy poles socketed into the trestle frame, canvas tied
to the poles with visible tie points, not fused to them), moves (A-frames
imply a folding joint at the cross), gripped (side rail as a hand rail along
the front), fixed (canopy pole feet sit in a socket/bracket), weathered
(canvas sag/fold under its own tension), maintained (crates stacked, not
melted into the boards).

## Asset 3: crate — crate.glb

**What it is:** A slatted wooden produce crate.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (boards nailed to corner battens, visible
nail heads, gaps between boards), supported (battens are the load path at the
corners, thicker than the slats), gripped (hand-holes cut through both end
boards), fixed (corner battens proud of the slat faces), weathered (edge
wear/bevel on the batten corners).

## Asset 4: barrel — barrel.glb

**What it is:** A wooden cask/barrel with iron hoops.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (staves meet edge to edge around the
bulge, chime bevel at both ends where the head sits in the croze groove),
supported/fixed (3 iron hoops standing proud of the stave surface at chime,
quarter and bilge), moves/opens (bung hole on the bilge), weathered (hoop
rust bleed, stave grain direction).

## Asset 5: sack-pile — sacks.glb

**What it is:** A pile of 2-3 slumped jute sacks with tied necks.

**Functional parts planned:** Joined (tied neck = twist + wrap read as
geometry, not a texture), supported (sacks slump and flatten where they rest
on the ground and on each other — different silhouette per sack), weathered
(creases at every contact point), material named `cloth` per brief override.

## Verdicts (filled in after render review)

### Asset 1: market — sandbox/assets/models/market.glb — PASS

Renders: `blender/renders/B1/market_face.png`, `market_34.png`,
`market_detail.png`, `market_ctx.png`, `market_interior.png` (5th frame,
beyond COMMON's 3-minimum, added because the brief specifically wants trusses
"visible from inside" and no exterior frame shows them). Tris: 38,556 (< 60k
building budget).

Fixes made after the first full clay pass, from reviewing the renders (not
guessing): the original `cam_detail` camera showed a blank pier face with
zero information — repositioned twice (once after fixing entrance geometry
invalidated the first fix) until it actually shows the gate post, hinge
plates, threshold and arch soffit together. The entrance was originally 10.6 m
clear (piers eating into the brief's 12 m figure) — corrected the jamb pier
centres so the clear opening is exactly 12.0 m, matching the brief. Interior
columns originally stood at bay *centres*, 6 m from the nearest truss, with
their bracket flare topping out under empty air — moved onto the same x as
the truss stations so the bracket physically overlaps the truss bottom tie.
The roof met the wall with zero eave overhang and the gutter sat inside the
wall face — added a real 0.8 m overhang continuing the roof's own pitch, with
the gutter and downpipes moved to the new drip edge.

**Stranger answers, from the frames alone:**
- *What is it?* A large covered market hall — glass-and-iron gable roof over
  an open brick arcade, with a grand arched entrance at each end (`market_face`,
  `market_ctx`).
- *How is it built?* Brick piers on a plinth carry segmental arches along the
  open sides; inside, iron columns with a visible base plate, shaft, flared
  capital and bracket carry the roof trusses at mid-span; the roof is a grid
  of iron glazing bars framing a glazed ridge lantern (`market_interior`,
  `market_34`).
- *How is it used?* You walk in through the 12 m arched entrance, past iron
  gate posts with hinge plates and over a worn threshold step, into an open
  hall lit through the glass roof (`market_detail`, `market_face`).

Not fully resolved: the interior columns' exact structural logic (why the
roof needs mid-span support at y=±10 specifically) is my own interpretation,
since the brief states the column position but not the reason; a real
Victorian hall this wide plausibly would need it. The gable-end radiating
glazing pattern is a simplification of the reference plate's ornate ironwork,
matching function (real glazing bars) but not the plate's decorative scrollwork
density — printed brief figures were followed over the plate's aesthetic
detail per BRIEF-COMMON's own rule.

### Asset 2: market-stall — sandbox/assets/models/stall.glb — PASS

Renders: `stall_face.png`, `stall_34.png`, `stall_detail.png`. Tris: 4,988
(< 8k prop budget).

**Stranger answers:** *What is it?* A trestle market stall with a canvas
awning over a plank counter. *How is it built?* Two X-shaped trestle legs
with a visible peg at the crossing point and a low stretcher rail, topped by
gapped boards; canopy poles socket into iron brackets at the trestle heads
(`stall_detail`). *How is it used?* Goods (crates) sit on the counter under
the canopy, out of the rain (`stall_face`).

Caveat: the sagging canvas is built from flat, unrotated panel segments
rather than a continuously curved surface, so at a grazing angle the joints
between segments show as faint seams rather than one smooth drape. It reads
correctly as fabric sagging under tension at the render distances used here.

### Asset 3: crate — sandbox/assets/models/crate.glb — PASS

Renders: `crate_face.png`, `crate_34.png`, `crate_detail.png`. Tris: 4,088
(< 8k prop budget; was 11,128 before nail heads were changed from icospheres
to cheap cones and the bevel modifier dropped to 1 segment — re-rendered

**Stranger answers:** *What is it?* A slatted wooden produce crate. *How is
it built?* Boards nailed to four corner battens, proud nail heads visible at
every board-batten junction, gaps between boards for ventilation
(`crate_detail`). *How is it used?* A hand-hole slot cut through the top end
boards lets you carry it (`crate_34`).

### Asset 4: barrel — sandbox/assets/models/barrel.glb — PASS

Renders: `barrel_face.png`, `barrel_34.png`, `barrel_detail.png`. Tris: 2,156
(< 8k prop budget; was 11,948 before the per-stave groove geometry was
collapsed from 5 short segments per stave to 1 continuous rod per stave, and
bevel segments dropped to 1 — re-rendered after the cut, no visible quality
loss).

**Stranger answers:** *What is it?* A wooden cask with iron hoops. *How is it
built?* Staves bulge out toward the middle (the bilge) and taper at both
rims (the chime), held by 3 hoops standing proud of the wood, with visible
vertical stave-joint lines (`barrel_face`). *How is it used?* A proud bung
plug on the belly is where it would be tapped (`barrel_detail`).

### Asset 5: sack-pile — sandbox/assets/models/sacks.glb — PASS (weakest asset)

Renders: `sacks_face.png`, `sacks_34.png`, `sacks_detail.png`. Tris: 4,016
(< 8k prop budget). Material is named `cloth` per the brief's explicit
override (not `plaster`, which is what other props use for cloth-like
surfaces).

**Stranger answers:** *What is it?* A small pile of sacks. *How is it built?*
Each sack has a bulging, ribbed body and a gathered, tied neck; the pile is
three sacks lying at different steep tilts so they read as slumped rather
than standing upright (`sacks_face`, `sacks_detail`). *How is it used?* The
tied neck is where it would be carried or poured from (`sacks_detail`).

Honest caveat, since a false PASS is worse than a stated weakness: this is
the hardest asset in the batch to fake with primitive geometry, and it shows.
The first version read as standing onions with a spike on top, not sacks —
fixed by increasing the tilt to 42-68° so the bodies visibly lie down instead
of stand, and blunting the neck cone into a knob. The remaining weakness is
that the "creases" are stepped facets from the stacked-frustum profile, not
sculpted fabric folds — they read as lumpy/ribbed rather than woven cloth.
If this needs another pass, the fix is a proper multi-lobe deform (e.g.
several overlapping squashed spheres per sack instead of one lathed profile)
rather than more tilt.
