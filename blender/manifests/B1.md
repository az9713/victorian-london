## market-stall — sandbox/assets/models/stall.glb — round-4 fix applied
**What it is:** a collapsible trestle market stall — two A-frame trestles, a
plank counter, and a canvas awning tied to two poles, holding produce crates.

clip/clamp hardware ("washers/nuts," then "two stacked rows of blocky rounded
rectangular segments") for two compounding reasons: (a) the tie was built from

a round cross-section used a CHAIN of short straight rod segments, which facets

## sack-pile — sandbox/assets/models/sacks.glb — round-4 fix applied
**What it is:** a pile of 2-3 slumped jute sacks with tied, gathered necks,
creased and mutually deformed at every contact point.
**Round-4 fix (fixlist item 3a — ground-contact creases):** the body surfaces
were smooth on the underside, reading as pillows resting on nothing. Added a
periodic radius scallop confined to the true underside (weighted by distance
past the equator) that breaks the flattened ground-contact band into separated
lobes with a crease between each — visible as a tread-like ridge pattern along
each sack's base in `sacks_face.png`/`sacks_34.png`. This is a per-vertex radius
multiplier on rings that already exist, at zero added triangle cost. Each sack
gets its own phase offset so the three creases don't repeat identically.
**Round-4 fix (fixlist item 3b — mutual squash):** round 3's dents only pressed
DOWN into sacks 1/2 from sack 3's weight; sack 3 itself had no matching dent
where its own underside meets the mounds of 1/2, so the contact read as simple
overlap. Added `dents3` (underside dents on sack 3 at both its tail end, toward
sack 1, and its shoulder end, toward sack 2), and strengthened the existing dent
formula with a difference-of-gaussians rim (a slight raised flank around each
dip) so the boundary between two touching bodies shows a fold line rather than a
smooth push, and pulled the dip amounts up so surfaces meet closer to tangentially
instead of visibly interpenetrating.
**Round-4 fix (fixlist item 3c — neck terminus):** the neck previously read as
"a thin curved rod ending in a blob" because (a) the taper toward the tie point
was a long, perfectly round, needle-thin wire, and (b) the anisotropic flatten
that was meant to fix this only started AFTER the tie point, so the dominant
visible length was still round. This round: reduced the pre-tie taper severity
(round wire is now thicker and shorter-reading), applied the width-grows/
height-shrinks flatten starting immediately at the base of the neck rather than
after the collar, shortened the overall neck run (0.30 -> 0.22), cut the upward
rise near to zero (0.20 -> 0.03) and increased the downward droop (0.10 -> 0.22)
so the end flops down and sideways against gravity instead of arcing up like a
handle. The result is a flat, faceted, drooping fold of cloth, not a round rod
with a ball on the end — a structurally different silhouette from the failure
description, not a decorated tip on the same shape.
**Tri count:** 7,548 of the 8,000 prop budget (down from 7,992 at round 3 — the
neck rework removed some ring density that the ground-contact scallop and
mutual-squash dents didn't need to replace, since both are free per-vertex
radius multipliers).
**Provenance:** `sacks.blend` re-saved 19:01:13, `sacks.glb` re-exported
immediately after, all three renders re-rendered 19:01:27-19:01:54 (all postdate
the blend).
**Pure-black scan:** all three delivered frames — zero sub-0.003 pixels.
**Renders:** face → `renders/sacks_face.png` · three-quarter →
`renders/sacks_34.png` · detail (sack 1's neck: pinch, proud tie collar, flopped
cloth end) → `renders/sacks_detail.png`
**Stranger's three answers, from the frames alone:** What — a small pile of jute
sacks. How built — each sack is a long slumped pillow shape with a scalloped,
creased base where it presses into the ground and its neighbours, a pinched neck
with a proud tie band, and a flat drooping fold of cloth where the tied end flops
over. How used — filled with produce, tied shut at the neck, and dumped in a
loose pile for a market stall, the top sack visibly settling into and deforming
the two beneath it.
**Verdict:** all three sub-items of fixlist item 3 addressed with new geometry
(creases, mutual dents, reshaped neck). The neck fix is a proportion/topology
change, not tip decoration, per the fixlist's explicit instruction. Fresh judge
scores this, not this manifest.
