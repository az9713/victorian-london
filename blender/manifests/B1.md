## crate — sandbox/assets/models/crate.glb — REBUILT this round (nail-head fix)

**What it is:** a wooden produce crate — slatted boards with corner battens and
nail heads at every board/batten joint.

### Fixlist item 2 — manifest overclaim, root cause found and fixed
The r7 judge found `blender/manifests/B1.md` claimed "nail heads along every
batten" while `crate_detail.png` did not show them: "the crop shows a raised
bracket/joint block, no distinct fastener heads are resolvable."

**Route taken: the nail heads DO exist in the geometry** — `add_crate()` in
`build_crate.py` places 8 nail cones per board row (4 on the long/front-back
boards, 4 on the short/end boards), at every one of the crate's board rows, at
both corner battens. The claim of "along every batten" was geometrically true.
What was wrong was HOW they were built, not whether: `add_nail()`'s cone used
`bmesh.ops.create_cone`'s default depth axis (Z / straight up), which is correct
for a nail head on a HORIZONTAL surface but wrong here — every nail in this
crate sits on a VERTICAL board face. A Z-axis cone on a vertical face presents
its narrow SIDE profile to a face-on camera, not its round cap — which is
exactly the "raised bracket/joint block, no distinct fastener heads resolvable"
the r7 judge saw. Confirmed by re-reading `crate_detail.png` before touching
geometry: the crop is centred on the hand-hole (y=0), and the nails sit near the
corner battens (y=+-0.173m) at the frame edges anyway, compounding the problem.

**Fix, in `build_crate.py`:**
1. `add_nail()` now takes an `axis` param ('x'/'y'/'z') and rotates the cone
   after creation so its cap faces OUT of whichever board face it's driven
   into — 'y' for the long (front/back) boards' nails, 'x' for the short (end)
   boards' nails. This is a geometry change (nail orientation), so the crate
   was fully rebuilt, re-rendered (all 4 frames), and re-exported.
2. Added a dedicated `crate_nails.png` crop (`cam_nails`, position
   `(hx+0.42, hy+0.30, 0.20)`, target `(hx-0.01, hy-0.05, 0.145)`, lens 85) aimed
   straight at one corner batten, close enough to fill the frame with the
   batten and the 2 nail rows nearest it, from both adjacent board faces —
   this is the frame that actually proves the claim, not `crate_detail.png`
   (which stays framed on the hand-hole, its own separate claim, unchanged).
**Nail count, this pass, read directly from `crate_nails.png`:** 4 distinct
round nail-head caps, each with its own cast shadow, visible in one frame — 2
on the near/left board face (top row + bottom row) and 2 on the right board
face at the same two rows. This crate has `n_boards = 4` board rows, so with
2 nails per row per adjacent face, every board row has a countable nail on
each side of the batten; `crate_nails.png` crops to 2 of the 4 rows to keep
the nails large enough to resolve individually.
Slats, battens and hand-holes were **not touched** — `crate_face.png`/
`crate_34.png` (unchanged framing) and `crate_detail.png` (hand-hole,
unchanged framing) still show the same board/gap/batten/hand-hole geometry as
the passing round-1 build; the hand-hole is still a closed rectangle (strip
above + strip below + two side segments), confirmed by re-rendering it this
pass (all geometry was rebuilt from the same `add_crate()` call, only

**Functional parts modelled:** joined (boards with real thickness and gaps,

## sack-pile — sandbox/assets/models/sacks.glb — round-8 body resolution + lumpy contents

**What it is:** a pile of 3 slumped, filled cloth sacks with tied, gathered
necks, lumpy irregular-creased bodies (not smooth pillows), and folds wherever
the cloth presses against its own filling, the ground, or another sack. The
neck terminus (pinch, cord-wrap collar, flared tied end) — passing since round
5 — was **not touched** this round: the neck branch of `ring_profile()` (t>1)
is byte-for-byte unchanged.

### Fixlist item 1 — the folds existed, they died at viewing distance. Root cause corrected.
The r7 judge's finding: at `sacks_face.png`/`sacks_34.png` (the wide,
viewer-height frames a player actually judges from) only 0-1 faint shading
transitions with a real cast shadow were countable, reading as "smooth lumpy
pillows or dough," even though `sacks_detail.png` showed real creases up close.

**The r7 fixlist's own stated cause was WRONG, and this round's investigation
found the actual one — recorded here honestly rather than silently fixed:**
the fixlist asserted "sacks.glb is 5,088 tris against an 8,000 budget... the
r7 brief warned that decimating a simulated mesh can smooth the folds back
out, and that is what the frames show." Checking `build_sacks.py` before
touching anything: the decimate block only runs `if tri_count > 7500`. The r7
build used `n_ring_body=22/16/16`, `n_ring_neck=13/8/8`, `seg=32/24/24` per
sack; working the loft's own quad-ring arithmetic by hand for those exact
parameters gives 2,496 / 1,296 / 1,296 tris per sack, summing to exactly
5,088 — the number r7 reported for the exported GLB. That match means the
joined mesh was already at 5,088, under the 7,500 threshold, before the
decimate check ever ran: **the block never fired, nothing was ever
decimated.** (I did not observe an r7 console log with these per-sack
numbers printed — this is a derivation from the code's own parameters,
cross-checked against r7's reported exported tri count, not a quoted log.)
The mesh was simply BORN low-poly. The fixlist's prescription (land near
7,500-7,900 tris) was still correct, but the mechanism was resolution, not
decimation.

**Fixes applied, in `build_sacks.py`:**
1. **Resolution raised, not decimated.** `n_ring_body`/`n_ring_neck`/`seg`
   raised from 22/13/32 to 30/15/38 (sack 1) and from 16/8/24 to 22/11/28
   (sacks 2 and 3). The decimate safety clamp is kept but retargeted from
   7,500 to 7,900 tris and, as before, does not fire unless the sim mesh
   itself exceeds it — this round it doesn't (joined pre-decimate tri count:
   **7,756**, confirmed identical after re-importing the exported
   `sacks.glb` fresh and summing `len(polygon.vertices) - 2` per face: still
   **7,756**, i.e. under the 8,000 budget with only 244 tris spare, spending
   the 2,912 the r7 fixlist correctly identified as unspent).
2. **Lumpy CONTENTS collision body (new `lump_offset()`/`make_lumps()`).** The
   sack body docstring always described the contents as "a lumpy rounded-box,"
   but the code built the SAME smooth loft used for the cloth envelope, just
   scaled down — a perfectly smooth, rotationally regular collision mass. That
   symmetry is what let the cloth settle by uniform radial compression with no
   asymmetric buckling to seed real creases. Each sack's contents body now
   gets 5-6 irregular low-frequency radial bumps (amplitude 0.20-0.40x
   `HALF_W`, different centre/width per bump, different random seed per sack
   via `jitter_seed`), applied ONLY to the contents (never exported) so the
   cloth genuinely drapes over an uneven mass instead of a smooth capsule.
3. **Mid-body-weighted jitter, not neck jitter.** The envelope's own
   symmetry-breaking jitter (previously a flat 0.006 everywhere t<PIN_T) is
   now weighted by a triangular profile peaking at t~0.5 (mid-body) and
   tapering toward the tail cap and the shoulder (`mid_body_boost=2.6`,
   jitter base raised 0.006→0.009) — raising amplitude specifically across
   the panels the judge is measuring, not the neck, which stays untouched.
4. **Compression stiffness dropped further** (5→2.5) and **bending stiffness**
   dropped (2.5→1.8) so the cloth buckles more readily under the now-larger
   excess material and asymmetric seeding, rather than resisting into a taut
   capsule.
5. **Tail-cap fan symmetry broken.** The tail cap was a single centre vertex
   with `seg` triangles converging on it — perfectly radially symmetric BY
   CONSTRUCTION, which is very likely why the r7 manifest itself honestly
   described `sacks_puckers.png` as "a fan of 4-5 creases radiating from a
   single pinch point... at even spacing," the same uniformity trap the ring
   generator hit six rounds earlier, by a different route. The tail centre
   vertex (envelope only, not contents) is now nudged off-axis by a small
   random offset (0.05x`HALF_W`, `jitter_seed`-derived) before the sim runs,
   so the fan's own triangles start unequal and any resulting crease is not
   perfectly even-spaced.
6a. **Sack 1 given a targeted second pass.** After the first full rebuild
   (items 1-5, 3-4), sack 1 (the nearest sack in `sacks_face.png`) read as a
   borderline "roughly 5" while sack 2 (nearest in `sacks_34.png`) read
   stronger at the same settings — plausibly because the same absolute
   lump/jitter amplitude reads proportionally larger on the smaller-scale
   sacks 2/3 (s=0.82/0.60) than on full-scale sack 1 (s=1.00). Sack 1 ONLY:
   lump count raised from 5-6 to 7-8 and the amplitude FLOOR raised (0.20 →
   0.24, ceiling unchanged at 0.40) via new `lump_n_range`/`lump_amp_range`
   params on `sim_sack()` — more countable transitions without pushing
   individual bumps toward the round-2 "boulder/rock" failure the r7 build
   comments already flagged as a risk of raising amplitude too far. Sacks 2
   and 3 use the original 5-6/0.20-0.40 range, unchanged. The renders below
   are from this second pass (final delivered state).
6b. **Sacks 2 and 3 remain draped differently** from sack 1 (different
   `body_deg`, different `jitter_seed` → different lumps and different tail
   offset) and are baked as colliders against sack 1/2 in sequence, as in
   round 7.
Pin verification (unchanged mechanism, re-run this round): `sim_sack()` prints
`PIN VERIFY max delta` for each sack — this round: sack1 `0.000000 m (OK)`,
sack2 `0.000000 m (OK)`, sack3 `0.000000 m (OK)` — the neck geometry provably
did not move during the sim, so it cannot have regressed from any of the
changes above.
