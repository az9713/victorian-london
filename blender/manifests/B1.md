## market-stall — sandbox/assets/models/stall.glb — round-6 canopy rebuild
**What it is:** a collapsible trestle market stall — two A-frame trestles, a plank

### Fixlist item 2a — canopy rebuilt as cloth, not a rigid moulded panel
The r5 canopy was a single continuous sine-curve sag end to end — the r5 judge
read it as a rigid moulded panel. Rebuilt in `build_canopy()`:
- **4 catenary sags — PASS, countable in `stall_face.png`/`stall_34.png`.** The
  canvas now sags between 5 support points (the 2 poles + 3 interior ridge points,
  `support_x`), each cell independently returning to baseline sag=0 at its own
  support (`cell_sag()`). Counted directly in `stall_face.png`: 4 distinct dips
  visible across the canopy's width, each with its own shadow line on the
  underside.
- **Hemmed edge with real thickness — PASS, countable in `renders/stall_hem.png`
  (new this pass).** Both long free edges (-y_half/+y_half) carry a rolled hem
  bead — one extra vertex per row, bulged 0.010 m past the plain sheet edge at
  mid-thickness, folding the flat knife-edge side quad into a rounded double-tri
  bead. `stall_hem.png` crops one sag trough face-on: the hem reads as a
  distinct, brighter, visibly proud rolled border running the full length of the
  edge, clearly thicker than the flat 0.018 m sheet plane behind it.
- **3 tie points/grommets along the ridge — PASS, countable in `stall_34.png`
  and `stall_hem.png`.** A small proud iron button sits on the canvas top
  surface at each of the 3 interior support x's (poles already carry the
  existing, unmodified rope-wrap ties) — the fixlist accepts "eyelet" as an
  alternative to "cord". 2 of the 3 buttons are visible as small proud discs at
  the top of `stall_hem.png`; a third is visible at a different sag peak in
  `stall_34.png` (the third overall is on the far side, occluded from both
  angles).
- **Budget note:** the first two implementations of the hem/tie geometry (a
  round rope-curve object, then a coarse box-beam chain) both blew the already
  razor-thin 8,000-tri prop budget (headroom was only ~130 tris even before any
  addition) — root cause traced to this object's mesh-wide Bevel modifier
  (`angle_limit=80 deg`) adding a chamfer to every edge over that threshold, which
  multiplied the cost of any new sharp-cornered geometry instead of the modifier
  being "free" the way it looked on paper. Fix: fold the hem bead and grommet
  buttons directly into the sheet's own vertex grid / existing bolt-nub technique
  (both already inside the working budget) rather than spawning new separate
  curve objects, and drop the sheet resolution to `n_steps=8` (2 segments per sag
  cell — a triangle bump, not a smooth curve, but still 4 clearly countable
  distinct dips at this object's on-screen size, confirmed in `stall_hem.png`
  and the wide shots).

### Fixlist item 2b — stall_detail.png re-aimed
The r5 crop (camera at `peg_x+0.15, -0.85`, lens 50) sat too close and too
square-on to the strap plate's own face — only one leg and its bolts were
visible, no second leg, a failed render per the fixlist's own wording. Root cause:
both trestle legs share the same world X (`x0`), so a camera viewing nearly
straight down that plane's normal foreshortens the X-crossing into one smear —
the wide shots read the X only because they view it OBLIQUELY (camera at world
x=0, trestle at x=+-1.02, y=-4.3, a large offset-to-distance ratio). Re-aimed to
`(peg_x+0.24, -1.15, plate_z+0.02)` targeting the plate centre (`plate_z=0.28`,
matching `build_trestle`), lens 40, preserving a similar obliqueness ratio at
detail range.
**2b PASS — `stall_detail.png`, verified by looking at the rendered frame.** Two
distinct diagonal leg segments are visible converging into an X directly above the
strap plate, with one leg continuing visibly down through/behind the plate to the
bottom-left of frame — both legs are countable in this one frame, and the plate
visibly straddles the crossing.

### Fixlist item 1 — sack BODY rebuilt (score 2, the batch minimum)
Rounds 2-5 all targeted the neck terminus; the r5 judge independently confirmed
it (and the stall's rope tie) now PASS, but named the real defect nobody had
touched: the sack bodies read as "smooth, continuous, worm-like blobs" — dough,
larvae, a curled animal — because the body cross-section was a pure ellipse with
only two, wide, un-rimmed gaussian dips as "folds". Rebuilt in `build_sacks.py`:

- **1a BODY SILHOUETTE (3+ facets/sack) — PASS, counted in `sacks_face.png`.**
  The body cross-section now blends toward a rounded-rectangle (`_rect_dir()`,
  `rectness=1.0` on the body) instead of a smooth ellipse — a flat-panel top, a
  flat-panel side, and a flat tail-cap front face are all counted as distinct
  facets on sack 1 in `sacks_face.png`, each separated by a visible shading
  break (meridional edges at the flat/corner transition are marked
  `edge.smooth=False` and split by a dedicated Edge Split modifier restricted to
  sharp edges only — `use_edge_angle=False` — so no angle heuristic can touch
  the neck, which never has a sharp edge marked on it).
- **1b CORNER PUCKERS (2+/sack) — PASS, countable in `renders/sacks_puckers.png`
  (new this pass).** Two proud rim-flanked "ear" bumps (`PUCKERS`) sit at the
  tail-cap's two bottom diagonal corners on every sack. `sacks_puckers.png` is
  framed straight down sack 1's own body axis at the tail cap so both corners
  appear symmetrically: 2 distinct proud bulges, one each side of the flat
  cap face, each with its own shadow-casting crease.
- **1c CLOTH FOLDS (5+, own shadow line) — PASS, counted in `sacks_face.png`.**
  5 rim-flanked creases (`FOLDS`, was 2, un-rimmed) run across each sack's body
  at irregular spacing and irregular depth (0.075-0.11, varied per fold to avoid
  reading as a uniform corrugated tube — the first pass at uniform depth/spacing
  did read that way and was walked back). Counted: 5 distinct ripple lines
  visible along sack 1's flank in `sacks_face.png`, each with a visible shadow
  edge.
- **1d SHOULDER (abrupt, not gradual) — PASS, countable in
  `renders/sacks_shoulder.png` (new this pass).** The body holds full width
  until `t=0.93` then drops steeply (accelerating `p**2` ease) into the neck's
  start radius over a short residual span, and the cross-section shape itself
  switches from rounded-rect to round circle over the same narrow band — a
  shape change and a size change at the same line. `sacks_shoulder.png` isolates
  sack 1's own transition, cropped away from sack 2's neck: one clear silhouette
  kink is visible where the faceted, ribbed body narrows through the cord band
  into the smooth round neck.
- **1e CORD BAND — PASS, unchanged (r5 fix preserved).** `COLLAR_HALF_T`/
  `collar_n` untouched; still a distinct rounded band, countable in
  `sacks_detail.png`.
- **1f FLARED END — PASS, unchanged (r5 fix preserved).**
