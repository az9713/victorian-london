Status: BUILT, round 2 (post-judge rebuild). 5/5 assets exported to
sandbox/assets/models/, clay renders in blender/renders/B3/.

**Round-2 note:** an independent judge round 1 FAILED this batch (minimum
score 2, on rookery) with a specific, itemised rebuild list per asset. All
five files are solely mine now (the dual-writer situation below is fully
resolved — no further collisions since). Every item on the judge's list has
been rebuilt and re-rendered; the per-asset sections below are rewritten to
reflect round 2, not round 1. The judge's cross-batch note ("no region may
render pure black") turned out to have two real, distinct causes, both fixed:
(1) common.py's clear_scene() never touched the world, so every render used

the viaduct's refuge niche, the gaslamp's frame rails, gy-flank's window-bar
collars) were built as a solid box embedded inside another solid box, sharing
a coincident face — a sealed, light-trapping cavity that rendered pure black
regardless of lighting. Both are fixed; see per-asset notes for what changed
and why.

## Asset 1: rookery.glb — functional-parts plan

Stranger line (target): "A decayed 1880s common lodging house, four storeys, two
blocks around a narrow yard."

- **Joined**: brick coursing (header/stretcher implied by brick material tiling),
  string courses between floors, parapet coping course, chimney stacks built up
  from the ridge with separate pots.
- **Supported**: floor-to-floor 3.5 m read via string courses; door steps carried
  on their own tread blocks, not floating; privy lean-to roof carried on a timber
  post + wall plate; standpipe bracket fixed to court wall.
- **Moves/opens**: doors sit back in a reveal with a frame proud of the brick;
  some windows shown boarded (planks nailed over sash, not a texture swap).
- **Gripped/carried**: door has a knob/pull; standpipe has a tap handle.
- **Fixed down**: door frames fixed into reveal; standpipe bracket bolted to wall;
  chimney pots seated in flaunching.
- **Weathered**: soot-black brick, missing coping bits on the parapet, boarded
  (broken) windows, worn step treads.
- **Maintained**: none visible — deliberately decayed/unmaintained, which is
  itself the read (a slum, not a kept building).

Plan: front block (−z face, doors every 5.5 m across 38 m = 7 doors), rear block
plainer/smaller windows facing courtyard, east court wall closing the gap,
courtyard privy lean-to + standpipe against the court wall, chimney stacks along
both ridges with pots, parapet with intentional coping gaps.

## Asset 2: viaduct-module.glb — functional-parts plan

Stranger line (target): "One repeating bay of a brick railway viaduct — a pier
carrying half of two brick arches either side, a deck on top."

- **Joined**: brick arch rings (2-3 recessed orders) at the springing/soffit,
  spandrel brick above the arch, stone string course as a distinct joined band,
  parapet coping as a capping course.
- **Supported**: deck structure (+9..+15) sits on the pier + spandrel walls;
  arch itself is the load path around the opening — springing sits on the pier
  shoulder, not free-floating.
- **Moves/opens**: none (static structure) — but the opening itself must actually
  be a through-opening (real geometry, walkable under it), not a decal arch.
- **Fixed down**: pier plinth wider than the pier shaft — footing, not a clean
  90-degree meet with the ground.
- **Weathered**: soot-black brick, weep drips under the parapet coping and
  string course so water doesn't stain the face below.
- **Maintained**: refuges/recesses in the pier faces (pedestrian refuge niches —
  a real maintained-railway detail, not a texture).

Tiling plan: module x ∈ [-11, +11], pier centred at x=0 (width 4, x −2..+2).
Half-arch each side must meet the neighbouring module's half-arch at x=±11 to
form one continuous 18.00 m span, 9.00 m clear height at the crown (crown sits
at the tile boundary, midway between two piers — i.e. the crown of each full
arch is AT x=±11 of a given module, shared with the neighbour). Springing
height and radius chosen and recorded in the geometry script; verified by
duplicating the module at x+22 and rendering the joint (this render doubles as
_ctx).

### Asset 4: gaslamp.glb — lantern door genuinely ajar

Renders: blender/renders/B3/gaslamp_face.png, _34.png, _detail.png.

Judge round 1 correctly caught a structural problem, not just a framing one:
the clay override replaces EVERY material, including glass, with the same
opaque grey — so a closed glazed door can never prove the burner exists in
any clay render, no matter how well it's modelled. Round 1's manifest claim
("burner visible in _detail") was unprovable by construction.

Rebuilt: the front pane is no longer a flat flush quad but a door hinged at
its pivot edge and rotated open (75°), leaving a real physical gap between
the door's new position and the frame's corner post. `_detail.png` is shot
through that gap at the burner's mantle/tip. Also fixed, same bug class as
the other three assets: the lantern's top/bottom frame rails were 4
full-length strips overlapping at every corner (a coincident-box light-trap
that produced round-1's black corner dots) — rebuilt as a proper
picture-frame construction (two full-length sides, two trimmed to fit
between them, no overlap).

**Stranger answer from _detail.png:** the door swung open on its hinge
(hinge knuckles visible), a hard shadow cast by the open pane, and the
burner rod visible standing inside — "you could reach in and light this."

**Verdict: rebuilt.** The burner is now provably visible (open door, not a
claim about a closed one); a few pixel-scale corner dots remain from
residual post/rail geometry and are cosmetic, not light-traps.

## Cross-batch fixes (common.py, apply to all five assets' renders)

1. **World lighting.** `clear_scene()` never touched the world, so every
   render before this fix used Blender's near-black startup world with zero
   ambient. `setup_render()` now sets a mid-grey world (0.45, strength 1.8)
   and raises Cycles diffuse/max bounces, so any genuinely recessed surface
   (window reveals, niches) gets real ambient fill instead of falling to
   black in low-sample shadow.
2. **Clay override robustness.** `apply_clay_override` used to `clear()` +
   `append()` a single material slot, leaving any face with
   `material_index` > 0 pointing past the end of the slot list (Blender
   clamps this, so it likely never caused a visible bug, but it was not the
   "override every slot" guarantee the judge asked for). Now replaces each
   existing slot in place.
3. **The recurring embedded-box bug.** Found and fixed independently on
   FOUR of the five assets (rookery door panels, viaduct niche, gaslamp
   frame rails, gy-flank bar collars): a solid box built with one face
   exactly coincident with another solid box's face, sealing a tiny
   light-trapping cavity that rendered pure black regardless of lighting.
   This was the dominant cause of round 1's "pure black" failures, not a
   lighting problem on its own — worth remembering for future batches: a
   pure-black clay-render patch is usually a coincident-geometry bug first,
   a lighting bug second.
