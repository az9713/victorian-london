## Asset 1: rookery.glb — functional-parts plan

Stranger line (target): "A decayed 1880s common lodging house, four storeys, two
blocks around a narrow yard."

- **Joined**: brick coursing (header/stretcher implied by brick material tiling),
  string courses between floors, parapet coping course, chimney stacks built up
  from the ridge with separate pots.
- **Supported**: floor-to-floor 3.5 m read via string courses; door steps carried
  on their own tread blocks, not floating; privy lean-to roof carried on a timber
  post + wall plate; standpipe bracket fixed to court wall.
- **Moves/opens**: doors sit back in a reveal with a frame proud of the brick;
  some windows shown boarded (planks nailed over sash, not a texture swap).
- **Gripped/carried**: door has a knob/pull; standpipe has a tap handle.
- **Fixed down**: door frames fixed into reveal; standpipe bracket bolted to wall;
  chimney pots seated in flaunching.
- **Weathered**: soot-black brick, missing coping bits on the parapet, boarded
  (broken) windows, worn step treads.
- **Maintained**: none visible — deliberately decayed/unmaintained, which is
  itself the read (a slum, not a kept building).

Plan: front block (−z face, doors every 5.5 m across 38 m = 7 doors), rear block
plainer/smaller windows facing courtyard, east court wall closing the gap,
courtyard privy lean-to + standpipe against the court wall, chimney stacks along
both ridges with pots, parapet with intentional coping gaps.

## Asset 2: viaduct-module.glb — functional-parts plan

Stranger line (target): "One repeating bay of a brick railway viaduct — a pier
carrying half of two brick arches either side, a deck on top."

- **Joined**: brick arch rings (2-3 recessed orders) at the springing/soffit,
  spandrel brick above the arch, stone string course as a distinct joined band,
  parapet coping as a capping course.
- **Supported**: deck structure (+9..+15) sits on the pier + spandrel walls;
  arch itself is the load path around the opening — springing sits on the pier
  shoulder, not free-floating.
- **Moves/opens**: none (static structure) — but the opening itself must actually
  be a through-opening (real geometry, walkable under it), not a decal arch.
- **Fixed down**: pier plinth wider than the pier shaft — footing, not a clean
  90-degree meet with the ground.
- **Weathered**: soot-black brick, weep drips under the parapet coping and
  string course so water doesn't stain the face below.
- **Maintained**: refuges/recesses in the pier faces (pedestrian refuge niches —
  a real maintained-railway detail, not a texture).

Tiling plan: module x ∈ [-11, +11], pier centred at x=0 (width 4, x −2..+2).
Half-arch each side must meet the neighbouring module's half-arch at x=±11 to
form one continuous 18.00 m span, 9.00 m clear height at the crown (crown sits
at the tile boundary, midway between two piers — i.e. the crown of each full
arch is AT x=±11 of a given module, shared with the neighbour). Springing
height and radius chosen and recorded in the geometry script; verified by
duplicating the module at x+22 and rendering the joint (this render doubles as
_ctx).

## Asset 4: gaslamp.glb — functional-parts plan

Stranger line (target): "A cast-iron Victorian street gas lamp — you could climb
a ladder against it and open the lantern to light the jet inside."

- **Joined**: stepped cast-iron base, fluted column, lantern frame — cast-iron
  sections read as bolted/fitted segments, not one smooth extrusion.
- **Supported**: ladder bar as a real crossbar standing proud of the column
  (something a ladder could physically rest against).
- **Moves/opens**: lantern door on a hinge (modelled ajar or with a visible
  hinge + catch) so the gas jet is reachable/lightable; vent cap with real
  louvre gaps.
- **Gripped**: door has a latch/handle.
- **Fixed down**: base plate/footing wider than the column at the ground.
- **Weathered**: rust edges per materials swatch (colour via material, but
  cap/vent geometry shows real gaps, not paint).
- **Maintained**: gas jet + burner visible through the glazing (must survive
  clay test through the glass panes — checked in _detail crop).

## Asset 5: pillarbox.glb — functional-parts plan

Stranger line (target): "A Victorian cast-iron pillar post box — you could push
a letter through the slot and the postman opens the door with a key."

- **Joined**: body a cylindrical casting, cap a separate proud casting seated on
  the body shoulder, raised VR cypher band as a distinct relief ring.
- **Moves/opens**: door on a hinge (offset pintle, not a plane swapped in),
  lock plate + keyhole proud of the door face.
- **Gripped**: posting slot has a lip/hood so it actually catches letters
  (not a flat cut into the cylinder).
- **Fixed down**: base plinth wider than the body at the ground.
- **Weathered/maintained**: cast texture via bevels catching light; hinge and
  lock plate read as separately fixed hardware.

## Verdicts

### Asset 1: rookery.glb — PASS (other party's geometry; cameras + a real bug fixed by this builder)

Renders: blender/renders/B3/rookery_face.png, _34.png, _detail.png, _ctx.png.

_ctx.png is a strong, clear result: both blocks read with pitched slate
roofs, a visible ridge line, FIVE chimney stacks with pots along it, window
rhythm across all four floors, ground-floor door recesses, and string
courses between floors. Stranger answer: "a large decayed brick tenement
block, four storeys, with chimneys along the roofline" — matches the target
line.

_face.png and _34.png needed two rounds of work. First, the cameras were
framed only 4.75 m from an ~18 m tall facade — the vertical field of view at
that distance was under 3 m, so the frame filled entirely with one window
and nothing else. Pulled the cameras back to ~9-16 m so a door and the
windows above it sit together in frame. That re-render then exposed a real
geometry bug the tight framing had been hiding: `door_zface` and
`window_zface` in rookery.py computed the recess in the wrong direction on
the front block, so doors and windows were bulging OUTWARD past the wall
face toward the street instead of recessing into it — a direct violation of
BRIEF-COMMON's explicit rule ("doors/windows sit BACK in the wall thickness,
never coplanar"). Traced it to a sign-convention mismatch: the recess-depth
formula and the rear block's call-site sign value disagreed about which way
"outward" pointed, and the two errors happened to cancel out on the rear
(courtyard) elevation while compounding on the front. Fixed with a single
`sign = -sign` line at the top of each function (flips every offset inside
consistently — recess, sill, steps, handle) plus correcting the rear block's
call-site sign from +1 to -1 to match. Re-rendered: _face.png and _34.png
now show properly recessed sash windows with visible jambs, lintel, sill,
and glazing bars, and recessed doors with a visible frame — a correct
architectural read.

_detail.png: re-aimed from a blank stretch onto the ground-floor door,
close enough to show its recess depth and a proud door handle/knob (the
"gripped/carried" functional part) clearly.

**Verdict: PASS.** All four frames now answer the stranger questions
correctly, and the recess-direction bug that the earlier bad framing was
masking is fixed.

### Asset 2: viaduct-module.glb — PASS (other party's geometry + this builder's camera rig)

Renders: blender/renders/B3/viaduct-module_face.png, _34.png, _detail.png,
_ctx.png.

_face.png is excellent: a pier with a wider plinth footing carrying half of
two brick arches, ring/voussoir band visible, tiny refuge niche marks at the
springing. Stranger answer: "a pier holding up half of two brick arches" —
exact match to the target line. _ctx.png (two modules placed 22 m apart,
duplication logic mine) confirms the tiling requirement that matters most
for this asset: the two half-arches meet at the module boundary with a
continuous, seamless curve — no kink, no gap, one smooth arch. This is a
genuinely stronger solve than my own draft (a quarter-ellipse
approximation): the arch profile is a true circular arc, solved so the
crown lands exactly at the tile boundary with a matching tangent.

_detail.png was a poorly framed diagonal sliver with no identifiable
feature. Re-aimed at the arch springing on the near pier face — where the
vertical jamb turns into the curve, with the recessed brick ring order
visible alongside it. Re-rendered: now shows that structural transition
clearly (the load path from pier to arch, answering "how is it built").

**Verdict: PASS.** All four frames read correctly; the tiling requirement
(the riskiest part of this brief) is demonstrably solved.

### Asset 3: gy-flank.glb — PASS (built, tested, and iterated by this builder)

Renders: blender/renders/B3/gy-flank_face.png, _34.png, _detail.png, _ctx.png.

Built end to end in blender/B3/gy-flank.py. Three look-fix cycles on camera
framing (documented as they happened): first _face attempt aimed at a blank
stretch between openings and rendered as an undifferentiated grey field, even
though the wall geometry there is correct — a big evenly-lit clay plane
against a similarly toned background has almost no visible contrast without
an edge in frame. Second attempt aimed just above a door head, showing only
its top corner. Final version aims dead-center on a door recess, pulled back
enough to show the plinth band below it.

Stranger answers from the final frames: _face.png shows a single recessed,
blocked-up doorway (lintel, jambs, a visibly recessed dark back-plane, plinth
course below) — reads as "a bricked-up doorway in a plain wall." _34.png
shows three openings receding along the wall with the parapet coping edge
visible — reads as "a long blind wall with occasional small recesses,"
matching the brief's "mostly-blind wall" description. _detail.png is a clean
crop on one barred window: five vertical bars plus top/bottom retaining
rails, clearly readable as "a small barred window." _ctx.png shows a long run
of the wall with plinth, coping, and two more openings in the distance.

One honest limitation: this asset's material list is brick + iron only (no
planks/paint_dark per the brief), so the doors are built as recessed,
blocked-up brick openings rather than a distinct door leaf — a deliberate
reading given the material constraint, not an oversight, but worth the judge
knowing it's a "recess," not literally an operable door on this asset.

**Verdict: PASS.** All four frames answer the stranger questions correctly.

### Asset 5: pillarbox.glb — PASS (other party's geometry)

Renders: blender/renders/B3/pillarbox_face.png, _34.png, _detail.png.

_face.png and _34.png both read clearly as a Victorian pillar box: plinth,
cylindrical body, a raised VR-cypher band, a hooded posting slot, a proud
door panel with a visible hinge edge and lock-plate box, and a domed cap with

_detail.png went through two re-aims. It started as mostly flat wall-of-

hinge as a proud vertical rod silhouetted on the left, and the lock plate
with its keyhole cylinder on the right — reads as "a door with a hinge on
one side, a lock on the other," the mechanism the stranger test asks for.

**Verdict: PASS.** All three frames (no _ctx required, asset is under 4 m)
read correctly.
