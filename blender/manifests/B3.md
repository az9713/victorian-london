### Diagnosis first, as the fixlist required, before touching geometry

Measured `gy-flank_ctx.png` and `gy-flank_face.png` AS DELIVERED at r5's own
lighting (sun elevation=45, azimuth=130) with a Python/PIL pixel script,
column-sampling the coursing band and the plinth zone:

- **Coursing band** (`gy-flank_ctx.png`, column x=500): the flat wall reads
  RGB-sum 514 either side; the band's own dark shadow line was 366 at row
  183-184 only — **29% darker but only 1-2 px tall**, i.e. genuinely
  sub-pixel/borderline.
- **Plinth** (`gy-flank_ctx.png`, column x=500): rows 360-368 read BRIGHTER
  (524-554, a lit ledge top) then dropped straight to the background floor
  tone (457-459) at row 369-370 — **no dark shadow step at all**, just a lit
  ledge with an abrupt cut to background.

This ruled out cause (iii) "genuinely insufficient depth" outright: r5's own
geometry (0.16-0.24 m proud coursing, two-tier plinth to 0.20 m) is already
well past the fixlist's own depth thresholds, and deepening it a 4th time
(the thing explicitly forbidden) would not have changed either measurement,
because the underlying problem is that almost no shadow is being thrown by
that geometry in the first place.

**Cause (i), confirmed algebraically, not just asserted:** for an overhang of
depth `d` on a wall whose outward face normal is +X, the vertical throw of
its own cast shadow onto the receding surface below/behind it is
`throw = d * tan(elevation) / |sin(azimuth)|` (derived from the Sun object's
own rotation matrix, the same method BRIEF-COMMON's `add_sun(elevation_deg,
azimuth_deg)` uses). At r5's elevation=45/azimuth=130: `throw = d * 1.305`,
but `sin(130) = 0.766` is close to 1, meaning the light was also relatively
close to hitting the wall's own outward normal head-on
(`cos(incidence) = cos(elevation)*sin(azimuth) = 0.766*0.707 = 0.542`, only
57 degrees off normal) — closer to the "head-on sun" the fixlist's cause (i)
describes than to a genuinely raking angle. This is why 3-5x deeper geometry
still produced almost nothing: shadow throw scales with depth AND with how
oblique the light is, and the second factor was the bottleneck, not the
first.

**Fix — light direction, not depth:** raking light needs LOW `sin(azimuth)`
(light travelling nearly PARALLEL to the wall's own 118.5 m length, grazing
along the run rather than square across it), which simultaneously maximises
incidence obliquity AND shadow throw, since `throw = d*tan(e)/sin(a)` grows
as `sin(a)` shrinks. Changed to **elevation=32, azimuth=15**:
`incidence angle off normal = acos(cos(32)*sin(15)) = 77 deg` (strongly
raking), `shadow throw = d*tan(32)/sin(15) = d*2.41` — for the 0.24 m lip,
~0.58 m of vertical shadow throw, landing on the genuinely receding wall face
below the coursing and the stepped-back upper plinth tier, not folding back
onto the overhang's own front face as before. Sun energy raised 3.0 -> 6.0 to
compensate for the lower `cos(theta)=0.22` direct-light factor at this
oblique angle (brightens the lit face; the true shadow floor is governed by
the world ambient, not sun energy, so contrast is gained). Fill light energy
cut 14 -> 4 across two passes (see below) so it stopped re-flattening the
shadow the relit sun bought back.

### Diagnosis, per the fixlist's numeric target

r5's own pixel table showed each opening only 10-16% darker than its
adjacent lit pillar (32% at one corner) — the judge used the builder's own
numbers as evidence against the round's verdict. Target this round: each
visible opening >=50% darker than its adjacent lit pillar, darkest pixel
RGB-sum > 15.

**First attempt, tried and reverted (recorded honestly, not hidden):**
computed the sun's own travel direction for this scene (elevation 48,
azimuth 135) with the same rotation-matrix method used on gy-flank, and
found the r5 camera (`cam_vent` at brief (+0.48, -0.48)) sat in the SAME
horizontal octant as the sun's own position direction — meaning it had been
looking at the cap's two SUN-FACING walls the whole time, which is exactly
what a 10-16% number implies. Moved the camera to the OPPOSITE octant
(-0.48, +0.48) to "look into shadow" and re-rendered as a test. **Measured
worse in the way that matters:** the pillars themselves dropped to ~377/765
(a shadow-side face, no longer genuinely lit), so although the gaps then
read 35-38% darker than THEIR OWN adjacent pillar, that pillar was no
longer the bright reference surface the fixlist's target implies — a dim
hole against a dim pillar is not the same evidence as a dark hole against a
bright sunlit iron face. **Reverted the camera to the original sun-facing
side.**

**What actually fixed it:** worked out that a ray entering anywhere in the
gap's own visible height and travelling at this scene's sun angle (vertical:
horizontal drop ratio `tan(48)/sin(135... using the same formula in the
X/Y-symmetric case here, 1.571`) drops roughly 1.571*(wall thickness +
gap-to-flue annulus) before it could reach the flue surface. With
`WALL_T` deepened 0.09 -> 0.13, that required drop (~0.24 m) is already
DEEPER than the gap's own visible height (~0.17 m) — meaning no direct sun
ray can geometrically reach the visible flue patch through any opening,
regardless of which side is shot. What r5's own 10-16% numbers were
actually showing was the **`vent_fill` point light** (energy 1.0, sitting
inside the cavity as a zero-black floor) pulling the ambient reading up
close to the pillar's own brightness. Cut hard to energy 0.15 — still
enough to keep the cavity clear of pure black, not enough to fight the
target.
