pixels, which is exactly the judge's "registers as one faint line" /
"thin white band". No amount of reframing fixes a shadow band that's
genuinely too short to draw; this was **(B)**, not a framing problem.

### Item 2b — crown junction: geometry checked FIRST, in the .blend

The judge's own words: `viaduct-module_detail.png` "shows what reads as
overlapping / interpenetrating blocks near the ridge... needs a second
look for a possible geometry fault, not just a lighting issue." Per the
fixlist's explicit order, this was investigated in the geometry before
touching the camera.

**Method:** every voussoir wedge (both orders, both half-arcs, both
z_faces — 128 wedges total) was logged at build time with its own polar
description: arch centre, radial band [r_in, r_out], angular window
[a0, a1], and depth band [z_lo, z_hi]. A first attempt used a
Cartesian world-axis bounding box per wedge and found 152 "overlaps" —
but this was a false-positive from the test itself: a global-axis AABB
of a curved, off-origin sector is not tight, so its corners bulge past
the wedge's true shape and register as overlapping its radially-adjacent
neighbour even when the two only TOUCH at a shared boundary (confirmed
by hand: order2's outer radius, 13.72 m, is exactly order1's inner
radius). Rewritten as a proper polar-frame test — a pair only counts as
a genuine 3D intersection if its centre, z-band, radial band, AND angular
window ALL overlap with positive measure (`min(a1,b1) - max(a0,b0) >
1e-6`, computed pairwise across all 128 wedges, ~8,128 pairs). **Result:
0 true 3D overlaps.** The geometry is provably clean.

Having proved the geometry, the render artefact was then traced to the
camera: r4's `cam_detail` (`V(4.0, 5.9, -9.5) -> V(2.0, 5.3, -6.0)`,
lens 45) was oblique enough to catch a raking, near-edge-on view of the
proud voussoir wedges close to the pier corner, and their thin proud
faces' silhouettes visually crossed in 2D screen space at that angle —
a real optical crossing of two correctly-placed, non-overlapping 3D
objects, not a mesh fault. First reframe attempt (closer, still oblique)
reproduced the same crossed-diamond artefact even nearer the pier,
confirming it was angle-dependent, not distance-dependent. Second reframe
moved the camera to a flatter, more perpendicular view of the near
z-face (`V(2.6, 5.8, -11.5) -> V(2.0, 5.5, -6.0)`, lens 42) — the crossed
shape is gone; the frame now shows clean, individually countable voussoir
wedges landing on the impost band with visible joint gaps between them,
nothing crossing.

**Countable, `viaduct-module_detail.png`:** 6 discrete voussoir wedges
visible meeting the impost band, each separated by a real joint gap, no
overlapping/interpenetrating silhouettes anywhere in the frame.

**Stranger's three answers, from `viaduct-module_34.png` +
`viaduct-module_niche.png` together:** What — a brick railway viaduct
pier carrying two half-arch spans, with a stone-framed refuge recess in

landing on a stone impost band at the springing; the pier stands on a

Renders: `viaduct-module_face.png`, `_34.png`, `_detail.png` (reframed),
`_niche.png` (new), `_ctx.png` (tiling-joint check, untouched, still
seamless). Tri count: 15,156 (was 14,940; budget 60k).

**Verdict: PASS.** Item 2a rebuilt as (B) and evidenced in a dedicated

### Item 3 — vent cap: (B), and the approach itself was wrong for 3 rounds

Three rounds on this one feature: r2 absent, r3 shallow steps, r4 proud
ribs on a solid cap — the judge's own words on r4, "the ribs read as a
decorative stepped crown or gear shape, not as a countable opening with
shadow inside." The fixlist's diagnosis: "stop adding ribs to a solid
cap. A vent is a HOLE." Every prior round (r3, r4, and this file's own
comments) used a segments=4 CYLINDER approximating a square, which is
what produced the recurring apothem/corner-clearance bugs the file's own
history documents. **Rebuilt from first principles, axis-aligned boxes
only:** a true hollow square shell (4 walls, 0.03 m thick initially, then
0.09 m — see below) with real rectangular openings cut clean through into
a genuinely open cavity, a solid flue core inside so a gap shows a
shadowed surface rather than a straight sightline through to the far
wall's own openings, and a true 4-sided pyramid roof (built from the
cap's own axis-aligned corners, not a rotated cylinder) sealing the top.

**First render caught a second, more subtle bug (not visible until
rendered, which is why the render step still matters even after a clean
rebuild):** with WALL_T=0.03, the tunnel through the wall was SHALLOWER
than the gap was WIDE, so most lighting/viewing angles saw straight
through to the flue's own directly-sunlit face — one gap rendered
BRIGHTER than the surrounding pillars, exactly the opposite of "dark
void". Fixed by deepening WALL_T to 0.09 m (tunnel depth now exceeds gap
width, so the tunnel self-shadows the flue surface at the sun/camera
angles used here) and adding a small interior point light in the open
annulus between the flue and the wall (clear of both solids) as a
zero-black floor, not a brightening fix.

**Countable, `gaslamp_vent.png`:** 3 real openings per face x 4 faces = 12
total; 6 are visible in this one frame (3 per near side). Pixel-checked

| gap 1 | 452.7 | 501.2 | 10% |
| gap 2 | 438.0 | 519.5 | 16% |

| gap 5 | 435.3 | 500.7 | 13% |
