## Asset 1: church.glb

Stranger line: "A stone parish church — a tower with a broad columned porch
and a tall pointed spire out front, a long windowed nave behind it."

**Functional parts delivered:**
- **Joined**: stone plinth course at the nave and tower bases; cornice bands
  dividing the tower into 4 stages (podium/shaft/clock/belfry); portico
  entablature carried on 4 plain Tuscan columns as a real projecting stone
  slab; belfry parapet with 4 corner pinnacles; spire sits on its own coped
  top above the belfry.
- **Supported**: entablature physically rests on the 4 columns (real
  cylinders under the slab, verified in `church_face.png` / `church_34.png`
  where the shadow falls correctly under the slab); pediment triangle sits
  on the entablature; spire tapers continuously from the tower top to the
  apex (4-sided pyramid, not a cone snapped onto a flat roof).
- **Moves/opens**: main door is a real round-headed through-opening recessed
  behind the portico columns, with jambs, a curved reveal, a threshold, a
  centre stile, and a proud door-plate/pull (`church_face.png`); all 8 nave
  flank windows (4 per side) are real round-headed through-openings with
  jambs, sill, and a curved arch reveal — see `church_detail.png`, a crop on
  3 of them in a row, where the reveal shadow and arch head read clearly;
  belfry has through-louvres on all 4 faces built as angled slats with real
  gaps between them (`church_ctx.png`).
- **Gripped/carried**: door has a proud plate/pull.
- **Fixed down**: tower plinth is its own footing, distinct from the nave
  plinth; belfry corner pinnacles and the weathervane finial are socketed
  onto their bearing surfaces, not floating.
- **Weathered**: none — the brief's material list for this asset is
  stone/slate/paint_dark/glass/iron only (no soot), consistent with a
  maintained parish church, not a ruin.
- **Maintained**: clock face (proud bezel ring + recessed dial + 2 hands) on
  both the front and back tower faces, at the stage-2 cornice line.

**Simplifications / deviations (disclosed):**
- The 2 spire lucarnes (front/back faces) are built as small gabled dormer
  volumes with a recessed, slatted louvre panel on their face, not a true
  through-void — at this scale/height a full punched opening into the
  spire's thin slate shell wasn't worth the added risk for a detail that
  reads as a silhouette from any acceptance-render distance.
- No flank doors either side of the tower on the nave's east gable — the
  reference plate shows them, but the brief's own text only specifies the
  tower portico as the entrance; omitted to keep to the written brief.
- No gutters/drips modelled at the nave/belfry cornices (mentioned as a plan
  intention, not built) — the parapet-concealed-eave condition the brief
  calls for doesn't expose a roof edge that would need one.
- Weathervane finial: pyramid apex is exactly **+50.0** (matches the brief
  figure and the greybox cone exactly); the finial fitting on top adds a
  further **+0.30 m** (total mesh height 50.30), which I read as a small
  fitting on top of the specified apex, not a re-spec of it.
- Portico + entry steps project to x=+19.10, beyond the tower's own +15 face
  — an intentional, brief-consistent ground-level entrance projection, not
  an envelope violation (tower body itself stays within x 7..15).
- Small footprint overshoots (≤0.08 m) from the plinth/parapet projecting
  past the nave's z ±15.5 face — coping/plinth stand proud of the wall by
  design; the wall plane itself is exactly on the greybox line.

**Renders:** `blender/renders/B2/church_face.png`, `_34.png`, `_detail.png`,
`_ctx.png`.

**Stranger answers (from the renders alone):**
- What is it? A stone church with a tower, a columned porch, and a tall
  pointed spire (unmistakable in `church_ctx.png`).
- How is it built? The porch is columns holding up a slab and a triangular
  gable (`church_face.png`); the tower is built in distinct stages with
  cornice lines and a belfry of angled slats near the top; the spire tapers
  to a point with a small finial.
- How is it used? You'd walk up the steps, through the columns, into a
  recessed arched door; the nave's round-headed windows (visible in
  `church_detail.png`) show it's the worship hall; the belfry louvres show
  where the bells hang and ring out; the clock tells the tower is also a
  public timepiece.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## Asset 2: ginpalace.glb

Stranger line: "An 1880s corner pub — a fully glazed timber shopfront at
street level with a recessed door, plain brick rooms with sash windows
above, chimneys on the roof."

**Functional parts delivered:**
- **Joined**: brick coursing implied by material/UV tiling on the upper 2
  floors; stallriser plinth and fascia signboard band as distinct joined
  timber bands below/above the shopfront glazing; parapet cornice capping
  the brick; chimney stacks built up with a separate cap and 3 pots each,
  seated on the stack.
- **Supported**: ground-floor glazing sits IN real proud timber joinery (3
  large panes each subdivided by a 3x2 glazing-bar grid, mullions between
  bays) which itself sits on the stallriser — see `ginpalace_detail.png`
  and `ginpalace_face.png`; the gas lamp bracket physically cantilevers off
  the wall to carry a small glazed lantern box over the door, not a decal.
- **Moves/opens**: corner entrance door recessed in its own reveal with a
  4-light radiating transom fanlight above it and a proud handle
  (`ginpalace_detail.png` — the fanlight bars and handle both read clearly);
  upper sash windows (4 per floor on the front, 2 per floor on each side)
  sit back in a real reveal with sill, lintel, proud frame, and a meeting
  rail marking the upper/lower sash division.
- **Gripped/carried**: door has a proud handle plate.
- **Fixed down**: chimney pots seated on their stack/cap; stallriser meets
  the pavement on its own plinth kick, not a clean 90-degree join.
- **Weathered**: brick material reads dark/sooted per the brief's swatch.
- **Maintained**: fascia board is blank (no text, per brief) but carries its
  own cornice to shed rain, reading as a kept, trading pub front.

**Simplification / rendering note (disclosed):** under the CLAY acceptance
lighting the small, deep-set upper sash openings (1.3 m wide x 0.35 m deep
reveal) render as a dark interior against the bright surrounding brick —
confirmed this is a real lighting/occlusion effect and not missing geometry:
the same openings show a filled, correctly-shaped glass pane under flat
(shadowless) Workbench material shading, and even a full opaque-clay material
swap on every slot didn't change it (ruling out a material/transparency
bug). It reads the way a real deep-set window often photographs in bright
daylight — the proud frame, sill, lintel, and meeting rail are all legible
against it. The wide, shallow ground-floor shopfront glazing is unaffected
and renders bright with its glazing bars fully legible.

**Renders:** `blender/renders/B2/ginpalace_face.png`, `_34.png`,
`_detail.png`, `_ctx.png`.

**Stranger answers (from the renders alone):**
- What is it? A 3-storey corner building with a fully glazed ground floor
  and plain brick rooms above — reads as a Victorian pub/shop, not a house.
- How is it built? The glazing sits in proud joinery frames on a stallriser
  plinth, capped by a signboard and cornice; the upper floors are punched
  sash openings with sills and lintels; the roofline has a parapet with
  chimney stacks behind it.
- How is it used? The recessed door with a fanlight and proud handle marks
  the public entrance; the gas lamp bracket over it marks where custom
  arrived after dark; the chimneys show the rooms above are lived in, not
  just storage.

**Verdict: PASS.**
