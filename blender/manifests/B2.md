## Retraction — round-1 ginpalace lighting claim was wrong

The round-1 manifest (see below) claimed the dark sash openings were "a real
lighting/occlusion effect and not missing geometry," reasoning that an
opaque clay-material swap not changing the black region proved it wasn't a
material bug. **That test result was correctly interpreted as "not a
material bug" but wrongly concluded as "therefore lighting" — it actually
proves a geometric cause** (a fully opaque, unlit region that no material
swap touches is exactly what a duplicate coincident solid produces). The
judge caught this correctly. Root causes, found and fixed this round:
- Full-height solid backing boxes on every upper-floor wall duplicated the
  window helper's own spandrel-filling geometry at the same planes.
- Separately, in the corner entrance, the door and fanlight both called
  `add_rect_window_xface` across the *entire* wall-opening y-range instead
  of their own slice, so each call's spandrel logic painted a solid green
  box over the *other* opening — this is why the fanlight specifically
  stayed solid black through earlier lighting and standoff-depth changes,
  which is what confirmed it had to be geometry, not shading.

## Coincident-face disease — found and fixed in 6 places total, 4 of them this round

Round 1 already diagnosed this class of bug (two solid faces occupying the
same plane, rendering as a black seam/void under Cycles) at the column
base/capital and pediment/clock-bezel. This round found and fixed it 4 more
times using the same test each time — "does an opaque material swap change
the black region? No → geometric, not lighting":
- **Ginpalace, all 4 wall corners**: side walls spanned the full `X0..X1`
  while front/rear walls also owned the same corner column. Fixed by
  insetting the side walls' ground-floor return and upper pier lists to
  `X0+WALL_T .. X1-WALL_T`, leaving front/rear to own the corners (same
  pattern as the tower-corner fix below). Confirmed clean in
  `ginpalace_34.png`, `ginpalace_ctx.png`, `ginpalace_face.png`,
  `ginpalace_detail.png` — no corner stripes in any of the 4 frames.
- **Tower +x door-flanking piers**: originally spanned the full
  `TZ0..TZ1`, overlapping `tower_shell()`'s own -z/+z face boxes at the
  corners; inset to `TZ0+TOWER_T .. TZ1-TOWER_T` to match.
- **Pinnacle plinth vs. coping rail**: plinth started exactly on the coping
  rail's top plane; offset to `TOWER_PARAPET_TOP + 0.01`.
- **Tower parapet cornice vs. balustrade base plinth**: these were built as
  two separate solid boxes with an *identical* footprint, touching at
  `y = BAL_Y0` — a full-face coincidence across the entire parapet
  footprint, producing the checkerboard z-fight dither visible under the
  balustrade rail in the pre-fix `church_balustrade.png`. Fixed by merging
  them into one box (`cornice(BELFRY_TOP, BAL_Y0 + 0.10)`) instead of two
  boxes sharing a boundary. Confirmed gone in the current
  `church_balustrade.png` — the dither pattern under the rail is gone.

## Disclosed, unresolved minor artifacts

- **Balustrade corner slot (church_balustrade.png only).** A small dark
  patch remains at each of the 4 parapet corners, next to the corner newel
  post, visible only in this tight close-up crop. The mechanism is
  derivable directly from the geometry, not inferred from a test: the
  parapet's base plinth and coping rail are both built as *solid,
  full-footprint slabs* (`C.add_box(bm, PX0, PX1, ..., PZ0, PZ1, ...)` at
  lines 187/190 of `church.py`, spanning the tower's entire ~8x8 m
  cross-section, not a hollow perimeter ring), stacked with roughly a
  20 cm gap between the plinth's top and the coping rail's underside. The
  balusters fill part of that gap, but between them it is an open slot
  roughly 20 cm tall and up to 8 m deep, roofed on both sides by opaque
  stone. Ambient light physically cannot scatter more than a few tens of
  centimetres into a slot with that aspect ratio, so looking into it at a
  raking angle — worst at the corners, where the sightline runs diagonally
  deepest into the slot — reads as pure black regardless of lighting
  direction. Confirmed empirically: re-rendering the same crop
  (`qc_bal_ambient.png`, not part of the acceptance set) with both the sun
  and fill light removed, ambient world light only, left the patch exactly
  as black and exactly the same size — consistent with "no light reaches
  that depth," not with a directional shadow. It is symmetric across all 4
  corners and does not appear in `church_ctx.png`, `church_face.png`,
  `church_34.png`, or `church_detail.png` — only in the extreme corner
  close-up, where the raking sightline down the slot is visible at all. No
  further fix applied; closing the slot itself (e.g. a solid backing
  panel behind the balusters) would remove the "see-through" balustrade
  the round-1 judge specifically asked for.
- **Nave window jamb shading (church_detail.png).** A dark band appears on
  the *outer* jamb of window 1 (leftmost) and the *outer* jamb of window 3
  (rightmost) in the 3-window crop; window 2 (dead-centre, zero viewing
  angle to the camera) is completely clean on both jambs. This is the
  signature of view-dependent reveal shading, not a geometry defect: all 8
  nave windows are built by the same loop calling identical jamb
  construction, so a real mesh hole would appear on the *same* side of
  every window (as the fixed sun direction does in `ginpalace_face.png`'s
  uniform-left reveal shadows) — a mirrored, camera-symmetric pattern like
  this one can only come from the oblique jambs exposing their own
  shadowed reveal face at a steep angle, while the straight-on window's
  jambs stay lit. No further fix applied.

## Asset 1: church.glb

Stranger line: "A stone parish church — a tower with a broad columned porch
and a tall pointed spire out front, a long windowed nave behind it, with
small round-headed side doors either end of the tower."

**Functional parts delivered** (see fix-list items 4–7 above for the round-2
changes; unchanged round-1 parts retained): stone plinth courses at the nave
and tower bases; cornice bands dividing the tower into 4 stages; portico
entablature on 4 real-order Tuscan columns; belfry parapet as a real
post-and-rail balustrade with 4 corner pinnacles and corner newel posts;
spire tapering continuously from the tower top to the apex; main door
recessed behind the portico columns; 2 flank doors at the tower base
(`church_face.png`); 8 real round-headed nave windows with leaded glazing
(`church_detail.png`); belfry through-louvres on all 4 faces
(`church_ctx.png`); clock face on front and back tower faces.

**Simplifications / deviations (disclosed, carried from round 1, still
accurate):**
- The 2 spire lucarnes are gabled dormer volumes with a recessed slatted
  louvre panel, not a true through-void — reads correctly as a silhouette
  at acceptance-render distance.
- No gutters/drips modelled at the nave/belfry cornices — the
  parapet-concealed-eave condition doesn't expose a roof edge needing one.
- Weathervane finial: pyramid apex is exactly **+50.0** (matches the
  brief's greybox cone); the finial fitting adds **+0.30 m** on top of
  that (total mesh height 50.30) — a small fitting on the specified apex,
  not a re-spec of it.
- Portico + entry steps project to x=+19.10, beyond the tower's own +15
  face — an intentional, brief-consistent ground-level entrance
  projection; the tower body itself stays within x 7..15.
- Small footprint overshoots (≤0.08 m) from the plinth/parapet projecting
  past the nave's z ±15.5 face and the tower's own coping rail projecting
  0.05 m past the tower wall (`PX0/PX1/PZ0/PZ1`) — coping/plinth standing
  proud of the wall by design; the wall plane itself is exactly on the
  greybox line.

**Renders:** `blender/renders/B2/church_face.png`, `_34.png`, `_detail.png`,
`_ctx.png`, `_balustrade.png` (5 frames; `_balustrade` added this round per
the fix list's balustrade/pinnacle requirement).

**Stranger answers (from the renders alone):**
- What is it? A stone church with a tower, a columned porch, and a tall
  pointed spire (`church_ctx.png`), with small round-headed doors flanking
  the tower base (`church_face.png`).
- How is it built? The porch is real classical columns (base, shaft,
  capital) holding up a slab and pediment (`church_face.png`,
  `church_34.png`); the tower rises in stages to a balustraded parapet with
  pinnacles at each corner, then the spire tapers to a point.
- How is it used? You'd walk up the steps through the columns into the
  main door, or use one of the two flank doors at the tower base; the
  nave's leaded round-headed windows (`church_detail.png`) show the worship
  hall; the belfry louvres show where the bells ring out; the clock marks
  it as a public timepiece too.

**Verdict: PASS** (round-2 self-assessment against the fix list; awaiting
judge round 2).
