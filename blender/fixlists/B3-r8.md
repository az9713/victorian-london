### 1. Bricked-up doorway — make it read as a DOORWAY
The problem is silhouette, not contrast. A blocked door and a window currently
have the same shape. Fix the shape:
a. Give it DOOR PROPORTIONS — full door height, running down to the ground or to
   a threshold stone, so its outline is obviously taller than any window and
   obviously reaches the base of the wall. A square hole at window height will
   never read as a former door however dark the infill.
b. Give it a VISIBLE LINTEL or relieving arch above, proud of the wall face and
   wider than the opening, casting its own shadow.
c. Keep the infill set BACK inside the original jambs, and deepen the set-back
   until the reveal shadow reads at 5.5 m — the current 0.15 m is half the
   0.30 m reveal depth available. Use the full depth.
d. Add a threshold or step stone at its foot, worn, proud of the wall.

### 2. Boundary plate — make it read as a PLATE, not a window
a. Give it a shape no window has: markedly wider than tall, or oval/shield, with
   a moulded raised border and a plain recessed field.
b. Mount it visibly ON the wall face — proud of the brick, casting a shadow —
   rather than recessed into it like an opening.
c. Put it at a height and position no window occupies (e.g. beside the gate at
   head height), so position alone distinguishes it.
