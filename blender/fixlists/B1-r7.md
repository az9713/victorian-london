Six rounds have failed on two features, and the failure DESCRIPTION changed every

  sack body: rigid rod → faceted beak → smooth worm → uniform corrugated tube

independent judge still read "uniform, evenly-pitched concentric rings,

**The diagnosis: the METHOD is the ceiling, not the parameters.** Both features
are cloth, and both are generated procedurally from stacked rings driven by a
radius function. A lathe-style generator produces rotationally regular,
mechanically uniform geometry BY CONSTRUCTION. Real cloth folds are irregular,
localised and directional — they radiate from contact points, pinch at supports,
and never repeat. That is not reachable by tuning a ring generator.

**So stop generating cloth. Simulate it, bake it, and apply it.**
Do NOT deliver another ring-generator variant. If the sim route fails, say so
and stop — do not silently fall back to the old method.

## 1. sacks — simulate a filled sack (score 1, the batch minimum)

Method, per sack:
a. Build the CONTENTS as a collision body: a low-poly rounded mass roughly the
   volume of the grain inside — this is what the cloth drapes onto and what gives
   the body its flat-ish panels and corner bulges. It is never exported.
b. Build the SACK as a cloth mesh — a closed tube/bag of even quads around the
   contents, resolution high enough to fold (aim ~30-40 segments around; you will
   decimate later). Quads, not triangles: cloth solves badly on triangles.
c. Add a GROUND collision plane. Contact with it is what produces the scallop
   creases at the base for free — those creases were already passing, and the sim
   should reproduce them naturally rather than have them modelled.
d. PIN THE NECK. Put the neck-end vertex ring in a vertex group and set it as the
   cloth Pinning group, so the sim cannot move the neck geometry that already
   passes. Verify after baking that the neck is unchanged.
e. Cloth settings: use a cotton/denim-ish preset as a starting point, raise
   tension/compression stiffness until it stops looking like silk, and enable
   self-collision (a sack folding into itself is where real creases come from).
   If the bag needs to look filled rather than deflated, enable cloth Pressure
   and raise it until the panels bulge between the contact points.
f. Sim 60-120 frames until motion settles. Sacks 2 and 3 must be draped
   DIFFERENTLY from sack 1 — different starting rotation or contact points — so
   the pile does not read as three copies of one object.

HEADLESS BAKE — this is where this round will break if it breaks:
Do NOT rely on `bpy.ops.ptcache.bake*`; ops needing a viewport context fail in
background mode. Instead step the frames yourself and snapshot the evaluated
mesh:
```
for f in range(start, end+1):
    scene.frame_set(f)                 # advances the cloth solver
dg   = context.evaluated_depsgraph_get()
ev   = obj.evaluated_get(dg)
mesh = bpy.data.meshes.new_from_object(ev)   # baked, deformed geometry
```
Then build a NEW object from that mesh — that is the deliverable geometry — and
remove the cloth modifier, the collision contents and the ground plane before
export. Confirm the exported GLB contains only the sacks.

BUDGET: sacks must stay under 8,000 tris (it is 5,884 now). A sim mesh will
overshoot; decimate (planar or collapse) AFTER baking, and check that decimation
did not smooth the folds back out — that is the obvious way to lose this round.
Re-render and re-count after decimating, never before.

## 2. stall canopy — simulate an awning (stall scores 2 on this alone)
Judge: "a zigzag sheet-metal or folded-paper roof — dead-straight edges meeting
at sharp angular peaks and valleys, no curve anywhere."

a. Build the canopy as a flat GRID of quads spanning the supports (a ~40x40 grid
   is ample; decimate after).
b. Pin ONLY the vertices at the support points (2 poles + 3 interior) in a vertex
   group set as the Pinning group. Everything else hangs free.
c. Run the same cloth sim under gravity. The sag between supports must be a
   CATENARY CURVE produced by the solver — curved in section, not two straight
   lines meeting at a point. That single property is what separates cloth from
   origami, and it is what r6 failed.
d. The free edges should ripple slightly and unevenly. A perfectly straight free
   edge is the tell that it is still a modelled sheet.
e. HEM: after baking and applying, give the free edges a real rolled hem —
   extrude the boundary loop and bevel it, or Solidify with a rim. It must be
   visibly proud of the sheet at the crop distance. r6's hem was built at 0.010 m
   and rendered as a knife edge, so the judge scored it ABSENT. Make it thicker
   than you think it needs to be, then confirm in the frame.
f. GROMMETS: small rings at the pinned points, proud enough to count. Reshoot so
   3 are visible in one frame (r6 showed only 2, the third occluded).
g. Re-shoot stall_hem.png so the bead is unmistakable, and stall_face.png so the
   catenary curve reads.

BUDGET: stall must stay under 8,000 tris (7,976 now — only 24 spare). The old
procedural canopy geometry is being REPLACED, so delete it before adding the
baked sheet; do not stack the new on top of the old. Decimate the baked grid.
Watch the mesh-wide Bevel modifier (angle_limit 80 deg) already on this object —
r6 found it multiplies the cost of any new sharp-cornered geometry, so fold the
hem into the sheet's own vertex grid rather than spawning separate objects.
