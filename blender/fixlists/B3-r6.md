## 1. gy-flank — the wide-frame test, THIRD consecutive failure
Judge r5: "`gy-flank_face.png` is still ~90% flat grey wall with a tiny plank
door; I cannot see the claimed coursing band or the claimed two-tier plinth at
all in this frame." r4 said the same. r3 said the same.

**Three rounds on one test means the APPROACH is wrong, not the number.**
r5 already tried the obvious approach — deepen the geometry 3-5x, from 0.045 m
to 0.16 m coursing and 0.08 m to a two-tier 0.20 m plinth. The judge still
cannot see either. So deepening further is the fourth try at a method that has
failed three times. Do NOT simply increase the proud dimension again.

### Diagnose first, in pixels, before you change any geometry
There are three candidate causes and you must determine WHICH by measurement:
 (i)  LIGHT DIRECTION. A proud band only reads because it casts a shadow. If the
      key light is close to normal to the wall face, a 0.16 m step casts almost
      nothing and NO depth will save it. Check the sun angle relative to the wall
      normal in the scene. A raking key — low and strongly oblique across the
      facade — is what makes relief read, and BRIEF-COMMON already requires "a
      clear sun key + soft fill so bevels catch". This is legitimate render
      setup, not score-gaming: you are not brightening a score, you are making
      built geometry visible so it can be judged honestly.
 (ii) FRAMING DISTANCE. Measure how many PIXELS tall the coursing band is in the
      current `_face` frame. If it is under ~3 px, no contrast will rescue it and
      the frame itself is wrong (see the framing ruling below).
 (iii) DEPTH. Only if the light rakes properly and the band occupies real pixels
      is remaining invisibility actually a depth problem.
Report which cause(s) you found, with the measurements, in the manifest.

### The framing ruling — read this carefully, it is a real change
A long wall cannot be shot whole, at 1.6 m eye height, and still show 0.16 m
relief. That frame is not a view any player ever has: a player WALKS ALONG this
wall at a few metres. So the wide frame must represent the player's actual view.

`gy-flank_face.png` and `gy-flank_ctx.png` may be reframed to a REPRESENTATIVE
SECTION of the wall seen at walking distance — camera at 1.6 m eye height,
roughly 3-6 m out, showing several metres of wall including the door and at
least one window. Keep one pulled-back frame for overall massing.

This is NOT permission to crop until a feature fills the frame. The constraint
is hard: eye height 1.6 m, and a distance a person would actually stand at.
A macro crop of a coursing band is not a wide frame and will be judged as the
evidence crop it is. The test the judge applies is unchanged: from the wide
frame ALONE, a stranger says what the wall is, how it is built, and how it is
used. Three things a person does at this wall must be nameable from it.

### Countable targets in the delivered wide frame
- the coursing band shows a continuous shadow edge running the full wall width,
  at least 3 px tall, measurably darker than the wall face above and below it;
- both plinth tiers show distinct shadow steps at the base;
- the downpipe reads as a pipe, not a hairline — measure its width in pixels in
  the wide frame and report the number;
- at least one window reads as an opening with depth, not a grey dot.
MEASURE each of these in the delivered PNG and report the pixel numbers. "It
looks better" is what failed the last three rounds.

## 2. gaslamp vent — FOURTH round, and the target is now numeric
The r5 rebuild was structurally right: a hollow box shell with 12 real
through-openings and an interior flue core is the correct construction, and it
replaced the ribbed-cap approach as instructed. Keep that structure. It is the
APERTURE that is wrong.

The judge's finding, using the builder's own measurements: the openings read
only 10-16% darker than the adjacent lit pillar (32% at one corner). The
verdict: "reads as a ribbed/finned decorative cap, not air escaping through
holes — a stranger would say fins, not vents. The numbers the builder collected
argue against their own verdict."

That is the lesson to carry: your own pixel table was the evidence that the fix
had not landed. Read your measurements as a judge would, not as their author.

TARGET, measured in gaslamp_vent.png:
- each visible opening reads at least 50% darker than the adjacent lit pillar
  face — several-fold, not 10-16%;
- and the frame's darkest pixel stays clear of pure black (RGB sum > 15), so the
  cavity is dark but still evidenced.
Route to it: the cavity is not deep enough relative to the viewing angle, so
light is reaching the back wall. Deepen the tunnel, enlarge the aperture, or
angle the openings so the camera looks into shadow rather than at a lit interior
face. Add an interior baffle if that is what it takes to kill the bounce.
Iterate against the measurement, re-rendering each time, until the number is met.
