## What to build — a real 1880s London boundary wall
This is the flank wall of a burial ground. Such a wall is not a plain slab: it is
built in bays, it is buttressed, it is capped, it has been altered over decades,
and it has a way in. Add, at minimum, ALL of the following. Every one must be
countable IN THE WIDE FRAME gy-flank_face.png at walking distance — not in a crop.

1. **BUTTRESSES / PIERS at regular bays.** 4 or more along the visible length,
   each standing proud of the wall face far enough to cast a full-height shadow
   down the wall, each with a weathered set-off or sloped shoulder near the top.
   This alone breaks the slab into bays and is the single biggest win available.
2. **A COPING along the top.** A capping course wider than the wall, overhanging
   both faces, with a weathering slope and a drip underside so it throws a
   continuous shadow line along the whole wall head.
3. **A BRICKED-UP DOORWAY.** A former opening, blocked with brick set BACK from
   the wall face inside its original jambs, with the arch or lintel still
   visible above it. This is the single most Victorian, most story-carrying
   feature available: it tells a stranger the wall was altered, which answers
   "how is it used" better than any amount of coursing.
4. **GATE PIERS at the entrance.** The existing door gets a proper pair of piers,
   taller and wider than the wall, each with a moulded cap.
5. **One more sign of maintenance or age** — your choice, and say which you
   chose: a repaired patch in different bond, a wall-mounted boundary plate, iron
   railing stubs cut off flush in the coping, or a rubbing course.
